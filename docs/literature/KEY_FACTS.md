## 1. Critical field and Dreicer field (Connor-Hastie 1975, Dreicer 1959/1960): primaries read

Files: `Connor1975_runaway.pdf` (PDF p = journal page - 413), `Dreicer1959_runaway1.pdf` (PDF p = journal page - 237), `Dreicer1960_runaway2.pdf` (PDF p = journal page - 328). Equations read from page images, not from OCR. Connor-Hastie use Gaussian units (4 pi e^3 n lnL/(m c^2)); the SI form is n e^3 lnL/(4 pi eps0^2 m c^2).

**Connor-Hastie, Nucl. Fusion 15, 415**
- Eq. 1 (p415, PDF p2): nu(v) = 4 pi e^4 n lnL/(m^2 v^3). Eq. 2: v_c^2 = 4 pi e^3 n lnL/(m E). Non-relativistic critical speed from E = drag.
- Eq. 3 (p415): **E_D = 4 pi e^3 n lnL/(m v_Th^2)**, v_Th = sqrt(kT/m). SI: E_D = n e^3 lnL/(4 pi eps0^2 kT). Definition: the field for which v_c = v_Th.
- Eq. 12 (p417, PDF p4) and eq. 65 (p423, PDF p10): **E_R = 4 pi n e^3 lnL/(m c^2) = E_D kT/(m c^2)**, "below which no electrons run away". This is the field everyone now calls E_c (Rosenbluth-Putvinski eq. 1, Aleynikov-Breizman eq. 1, Choudhury PRL eq. 1 are all this expression). The paper's own order-of-magnitude: E_R ~ 5e-2 V/m for n ~ 5e19 m^-3 (p423).
- Eq. 27 (p419): alpha = E/E_D x (m c^2/kT) = E/E_R. Region V critical momentum, p > m c q_c with **q_c = 1/sqrt(alpha - 1)** (p423, below eq. 65): the critical momentum goes to infinity as E -> E_R. Normalised p = m c q, small parameter epsilon = kT/(m c^2) (p419).
- Non-relativistic runaway rates (E << E_D), p415-416: eq. 5 Gurevich S_G = (2/sqrt(pi)) n nu(v_Th) (E/E_D)^(1/2) exp{-E_D/(4E) - (2E_D/E)^(1/2)}; eq. 6 Lebedev S_L = 0.36 n nu(v_Th) (E/E_D)^(-1/4) exp{same}; eq. 7 Kruskal-Bernstein S_KB = c n nu(v_Th) (E/E_D)^(-3/8) exp{same}, c = 0.35 from the numerical fit of their ref [6]. The exponential is the same in all three; only the pre-factor differs.
- **Relativistic runaway rate, eqs. 63-64 (p423, PDF p10), valid E > E_R**: S_R = C_R n nu(v_Th) (E/E_D)^(-h(alpha,Z)) exp{ -lambda(alpha) E_D/(4E) - (2E_D/E)^(1/2) gamma(alpha,Z) }, with lambda(alpha) = 8 alpha (alpha - 1/2 - sqrt(alpha(alpha-1))), gamma(alpha,Z) = sqrt{(1+Z) alpha^2/(8(alpha-1))} [pi/2 - arcsin(1 - 2/alpha)], h(alpha,Z) (eq. 62) = {alpha(Z+1) - Z + 7 + 2 sqrt(alpha/(alpha-1)) (1+Z)(alpha-2)}/(16(alpha-1)). C_R(alpha,Z) is an "unknown constant of order unity in epsilon" (the paper does not fix it). S_R -> 0 as alpha -> 1 (E -> E_R). Z is the effective ion charge (Z_eff).
- Eq. 66-67 (p424, PDF p11): for alpha >> 1, S_R ~ S_NR exp{-(kT/mc^2)[(1/8)(E_D/E)^2 + (2/3)(E_D/E)^(3/2) (1+Z)^(1/2)]}, S_NR = C n nu(v_Th) (E/E_D)^(-3(Z+1)/16) exp{-E_D/(4E) - (2E_D/E)^(1/2) (1+Z)^(1/2)}. Relativistic corrections matter when (E_D/E)^2 kT/(m c^2) >~ 1 (abstract). Table I (p424) checks the Z dependence against Kulsrud et al.
- Caveat the authors state (p416): lnL should really be ln(Lambda m v_c^2/2kT) at the velocities of interest, a correction factor ~ (E_D/E)^(-E_D/(4E lnL)) that can exceed the (E_D/E)^(-3/8) pre-factor.

**Dreicer 1959 (I) and 1960 (II)**
- Dreicer I, eq. 20 (journal p241, PDF p4): e E_c = n m Gamma_e beta_e, Gamma_e = 4 pi (e^2/4 pi eps0 m)^2 ln(Lambda/p0), beta_e = m/(2 k T_e) (Dreicer II eq. 1, p329, gives the same). In SI: **E_c(Dreicer) = n e^3 lnL/(8 pi eps0^2 k T_e) = E_D(Connor-Hastie)/2.** Do not mix the two "critical fields": Dreicer's E_c is half of the E_D used by Connor-Hastie and by DREAM/CODE-type normalisations of E/E_D.
- Dreicer I, eq. 24a-b (p241): hydrodynamic (average-electron) criterion: the drift runs away only if E/E_c > Psi(1) = 0.43, with Psi(z) the friction function of eqs. 20-22c (Psi -> 4z/(3 sqrt(pi)) small z, 1/z^2 large z).
- Dreicer II (journal p329-338): the runaway probability is Q(V_b|tau) ~ 1 - exp(-lambda_1 tau) (eq. 32, p336); lambda_1 is an eigenvalue obtained only numerically (Fig. 7, p336, PDF p8: lambda_1 in units of nu_e vs transition-sphere radius V_b with E/E_c = 0 ... 0.316 as parameter; Table II, p337). **No closed-form rate in Dreicer; the exp[-E_D/4E - sqrt(2E_D/E)] closed form comes from Gurevich / Kruskal-Bernstein (Connor-Hastie eqs. 5-7).** Dreicer II eq. 33 (p336): critical speed V_t ~ (E_c/E)^(1/2) in units of the thermal speed.

**v1 secondary material kept** (from citing papers; not re-checked):
- Hesslow 2019 (`Hesslow2019_MMI.pdf`, p4, unnumbered, just before eq. 8): "(Connor-Hastie) critical electric field is E_c = m_e c/(e tau_c)", with the relativistic collision time tau_c = 4 pi eps0^2 m_e^2 c^3/(n_e e^4 ln Lambda_c) and ln Lambda_c ~ 14.6 + 0.5 ln(T_eV/n_e20) (T_eV in eV, n_e20 in 1e20 m^-3). n_e is the free electron density.
- Choudhury 2025 (`Choudhury2025_helicon.pdf`, p1, eq. 1): E_C = n_e e^3 ln Lambda/(4 pi eps0^2 m_e c^2). This equals the Hesslow form. The extracted text of Hesslow 2018 (`Hesslow2018_Eceff.pdf`, introduction) prints eps0 with exponent 3; dimensional analysis and the two forms above give exponent 2, so treat that as an extraction or typo issue.
- Effective critical field E_c^eff (Hesslow 2018, `Hesslow2018_Eceff.pdf`): defined as the minimum field at which the pitch-angle-averaged force vanishes, E_c^eff = min_E { U(p,E) = 0 } (eq. 22, p8). Closed form with partial screening, synchrotron and bremsstrahlung: eqs. 23-24 (p8-9), iterated from E_c^eff = E_c^tot, where E_c^tot is E_c with n_e replaced by the total (free plus bound) electron density (eq. 25). Accurate to within 10% per the paper. Numerical implementation named in a footnote: github.com/hesslow/Eceff. The abstract says E_c^eff is "drastically larger" than Connor-Hastie and exceeds E_c^tot.
- Stahl 2015 (`Stahl2015_Eceff.pdf`, p1-3): the effective threshold for significant RE growth exceeds E_c because of (1) the strong temperature dependence of primary generation and (2) synchrotron losses. Primary rate scaling quoted on p2: dn_r/dt ~ n_e nu_ee E^(-3(1+Zeff)/16) exp[-1/(4E) - sqrt((1+Zeff)/E)] with E normalised to E_D. Fig. 3 (p3) shows the ratio of CODE primary growth with and without synchrotron versus E/E_c, T_e and n_e for B = 4 T, Zeff = 1.5.
- Experimental ratio: Choudhury 2025 (p1) states that "some multiple of E_C, typically >= 5" is needed for significant RE growth. Liu 2018 (`Liu2018_kineticinstab.pdf`, p3): previous work gave a threshold of about 2 E_CH; the new self-consistent whistler-instability model gives 6.4 E_CH (also Fig. 4b, p9). Here E_CH is the Connor-Hastie field.

## 2. Avalanche rate (Rosenbluth-Putvinski 1997): primary read

File `Rosenbluth1997_avalanche.pdf` (PDF p = journal page - 1353). Equations read from page images. Gaussian units as printed.
- Intro, p1355 (PDF p2): E_c, eq. 1, = 4 pi e^3 n_e lnL/(m c^2); high-field estimate gamma_RA = (1/j)(dj/dt) ~ e E/(2 m c lnL); total e-folds gamma_RA t ~ I_p/(I_A lnL), I_A = m c^3/e ~ 0.02 MA (ITER gain e^50, present machines ~ e^2).
- Definitions (p1356-1357, PDF p3-4): tau = m0^2 c^3/(4 pi n_e e^4 lnL) = m0 c/(e E_c) (the relativistic collision time, = tau_c). Beta (p1357) = (2 gamma/(3(Z+1)))(E/E_c)^2, where **gamma here is the neoclassical (trapped-particle) function of eq. 11, gamma(eps) ~ (1 + 1.46 sqrt(r/R) + 1.72 r/R)^-1 -- not a Lorentz factor.**
- Eq. 7 (p1356): high-field, E/E_c >> 1: (1/n_r) dn_r/dt = (1/(2 tau lnL))(E/E_c - 1), formally Z = -1 (no pitch-angle scattering of primaries). Validity eq. 8: 2(Z+1) E/E_c >> E/E_c >> 1. This is the "C = 1" case Embreus 2018 quotes.
- Eq. 15 (p1357): joining formula (1/n_r) dn_r/dt = (1/(tau lnL)) (pi beta/2)^(1/2) (1 + 8 pi/(9 beta))^(1/2) as printed (limits eqs. 13-14: C = (2 pi beta)^(1/2) for beta >> 1, C = 3 beta/2 for beta << 1). Eq. 17: near threshold, (1/n_r) dn_r/dt = (1/(tau (Z+1) lnL)) (E/E_c)(E/E_c - 1).
- **Eq. 18 (p1358, PDF p5), the working formula, valid E >= E_c**: (1/j_RA) dj_RA/dt = (1/(tau lnL)) sqrt(pi gamma/(3(Z+5))) (E/E_c - 1) [1 - E_c/E + 4 pi (Z+1)^2/(3 gamma (Z+5)(E^2/E_c^2 + 4/gamma^2 - 1))]^(-1/2). "Z+1 replaced by Z+5 to match the known result of Eq. (7)". Accuracy claimed 5% at large field, systematically low near E_c (Figs. 1-2), better than 20% overall (abstract, p1355).
- Eq. 19 (p1358): with bound electrons, E_c = 2 pi e^3 (n_e + n_T) lnL/(m c^2), n_T total electron density "including free and bound electrons, n_T = n_e + n_b". As printed, n_e + n_T = 2 n_e + n_b, i.e. bound electrons enter with weight 1/2 relative to free ones (fully ionized limit n_b = 0 gives the usual 4 pi n_e). Later literature (Hesslow 2018/2019) uses the total n_e + n_b with full weight. Check before use.
- Eq. 22-23 (p1358) Langevin equations of the Monte Carlo model; lnL = ln(2^(1/4) m c^2 (1+p^2)^(3/4)/(hbar omega_p)), "lnL ~ 18 + ln p".
- Eq. 4-6 (p1356): the source is the leading-order Moller term, S = n_T delta(lambda - lambda_2) sqrt(1 - lambda b)/(tau lnL) (1/p^2) d/dp (1/(1 - 1/sqrt(1+p^2))); secondary pitch is fixed by energy-momentum conservation (eq. 5), lambda_2 b = 2/(sqrt(1+p^2) + 1).
- **Check of the DREAM NOTES table** (results/codes/DREAM/NOTES.md, case a, Z = 1, tau_c lnL = 33.43 s, gamma = 1): its "RP97 sqrt form" column is eq. 18 without the final bracket. With the bracket the RP rate is 4.9% higher at E/E_c = 6.54 (0.0727 vs 0.0692 per s), 5.9% at 2, 5.5% at 3, 5.4% at 5, 3.8% at 10, 2.2% at 20, 1% at 50. The DREAM kinetic / RP-eq.18 ratio then reads 0.49 (E/E_c = 2), 0.65 (3), 0.66 (5), 0.73 (6.54), 0.76 (10), 0.81 (20), 0.85 (50), i.e. the kinetic code is 15-35% below RP, not 6-24%. The (E/E_c - 1)/(2 tau lnL) column is eq. 7, valid only for E/E_c >> 1.

## 3. Aleynikov-Breizman thresholds and the synchrotron-limited energy: primary read

File `Aleynikov2015_thresholds.pdf` (PRL 114, 155001; PDF p = journal page). Equations read from page images. SI-style normalisation as printed: s in units of tau = 4 pi eps0^2 m0^2 c^3/(e^4 n_e lnL) (= tau_c), p in m c, E in E_c = e^3 n_e lnL/(4 pi eps0^2 m0 c^2) (eq. 1, p1), tau_rad-bar = tau_rad/tau = (6 pi eps0 m0^3 c^3/(e^4 B^2))(1/tau). Z = ion charge (Z_eff).
- Kinetic equation, eq. 2 (p2): dF/ds + d/dp[(E cos theta - 1 - 1/p^2 - (p sqrt(1+p^2)/tau_rad-bar) sin^2 theta) F] = (1/sin th) d/dth sin th [E (sin th/p) F + ((Z+1)/2)(sqrt(p^2+1)/p^3) dF/dth] + (1/tau_rad-bar)(cos th sin th/sqrt(1+p^2)) F. The last term is dropped "because tau << tau_rad" (p2).
- **Pitch-resolved picture**: lowest order, eq. 3-4: F = G(s,p) (A/(2 sinh A)) exp[A cos theta], **A(p) = 2 E p^2/((Z+1) sqrt(p^2+1))** (eq. 5). Pitch-angle diffusion balances the electric-field shrinking of the pitch angle: the runaways are a pitch-Gaussian of width ~ 1/sqrt(A) around theta = 0 (modal theta = 1/sqrt(A), <theta^2> = 2/A for A >> 1). Integrating over pitch gives a continuity equation in p, dG/ds + d(U G)/dp = 0 (eq. 6) with flow velocity **U(p) = -[1/A - 1/tanh A] E - 1 - 1/p^2 + ((Z+1)/(E tau_rad-bar)) ((p^2+1)/p) [1/A - 1/tanh A]** (eq. 7). I re-derived this: the E-term is E<cos th> = E(coth A - 1/A), the synchrotron term is -(p gamma/tau_rad-bar) <sin^2 th> with <sin^2 th> = (2/A)(coth A - 1/A). Numerical check of the transcription: at Z = 5, tau_rad-bar = 70, E = 1.8 the roots of U are p_min = 5.08, p_max = 13.68, matching Fig. 1 (p_min ~ 5, p_max ~ 14); and eq. 8 gives E_0 = 1.77 for the dashed curve (1.7 in Fig. 1).
- **Two thresholds** (p2-4). (i) Sustainment field E_0(Z, tau_rad-bar): U(p) < 0 for all p if E < E_0; for E > E_0 U has two zeros, p_min (unstable) and p_max (stable attractor); electrons below p_min slow into the bulk, above p_min flow to p_max and "accumulate there". E_0 is defined by the merger of p_min and p_max (dashed curve of Fig. 1). Fit, eq. 8: **E_0 ~ 1 + ((Z+1)/sqrt(tau_rad-bar)) / (1/8 + (Z+1)^2/tau_rad-bar)^(1/6)**, "better than 5% accuracy for 1 < Z < 30 and tau_rad-bar > 5". (ii) Avalanche-onset field E_a > E_0: with gamma_0 = sqrt(p_max^2+1) the primary energy and gamma_min = sqrt(p_min^2+1), the Moller cross-section (eq. 9) integrated over gamma_min < gamma < gamma_0 + 1 - gamma_min gives Gamma = (n_e sigma c tau/2) sqrt(gamma_0^2 - 1)/gamma_0 (eq. 10; closed form eq. 11). Avalanche threshold is **gamma_0 + 1 - 2 gamma_min = 0** (p4); for E_0 < E < E_a the avalanche rate is negative (large-angle collisions knock electrons below gamma_min) and the population is sustained without multiplying. Hysteresis: rising field from E < E_0 gives nothing until E_a; falling from E > E_a the avalanche stops at E_a but existing runaways persist while E > E_0.
- **Synchrotron-limited energy**: p_max is the stable zero of U(p). "The stable point p_max would not exist in the absence of synchrotron losses" (p3); synchrotron "sets an upper limit on runaway energies". The distribution peaks near the attractor (Fig. 3, p3, Monte Carlo of eq. 2). E_0 "should not be confused with the critical electric field E_c" (p3). Current decay: tau_min ~ mu0 I/E_0 (eq. 14, p5).
- Eq. 11 vs RP eq. 18: they agree "when the electric field is several times greater than E_c" and RP overestimates at lower field (Fig. 4, Z = 5, tau_rad-bar = 70, p4).

**Comparison with the DREAM finding** (`results/codes/DREAM/NOTES.md`, L2 case: B = 2 T, n lnL = 1.5e19, E/E_c = 6.54, Z = 1; DREAM kinetic steady state: modal gamma 16.8-17.1 at pitch 0.105-0.107 rad, mean gamma ~20; test-particle ODE: gamma = 13.13 at 0.136 rad). Computed here with eq. 7 (script run in the session scratch area, not kept in the project):
- The L2 case has tau_rad-bar = tau_s/tau_c = 1.2897/2.2285 = **0.579**. The A-B fit (eq. 8) and the dropped last term of eq. 2 need tau_rad-bar > 5 (for these n lnL that means B < 0.68 T). **The A-B quasi-equilibrium does not apply to the L2 case**; it is not a prediction for it.
- Formally evaluating eq. 5/7 anyway: U has a single zero, p = 10.34 (gamma = 10.39, KE 5.1 MeV), A = 67, modal pitch 1/sqrt(A) = 0.122 rad, rms 0.172 rad, <sin^2 th> = 0.029 (vs sin^2 0.136 = 0.0185 for the ODE).
- Direction of the offset: pitch-averaging increases the mean synchrotron loss, so A-B gives a lower gamma (10.4) than the ODE (13.13), the opposite sign to DREAM (17-20). Pitch spread alone therefore does not explain the DREAM bump. The width law theta_mode = 1/sqrt(A) = sqrt((Z+1) gamma/(2 E p^2)) gives 0.095 rad at gamma = 17 (DREAM: 0.106) and 0.108 at gamma = 13.13 (ODE: 0.136).
- Honest reading: the ODE is the zero-width limit, A-B is the pitch-equilibrated limit for slow synchrotron; L2 sits in the opposite regime (fast synchrotron pitch damping, which A-B drop). The discrepancy has to be settled by the 2-D steady state; the DREAM mode in gamma versus the mean (17 vs 20) already shows the distribution is broad in energy, which neither reduced model has. A direct test: rerun DREAM/CODE at tau_rad-bar >= 5 (lower B or higher n lnL), where eq. 7 should give p_max to a few percent, then step B up and watch where the A-B root departs from the kinetic mode.
- I could not reproduce the tau_rad-bar = 10 that Choudhury 2025 quotes for DIII-D shot 201948 (B = 1.4 T, n_e ~ 0.5e19): tau_rad(1.4 T) = 5.159/B^2 = 2.63 s and tau_c ~ 3.7 s (n_e = 0.5e19, lnL = 18), a ratio of ~0.7. So the DIII-D quiescent scenario is probably also outside the A-B fit range; their stated definition is "6 pi eps0 m^3 c^3/(e^4 B^2) (1/tau)".

**v1 secondary material kept** (force forms for the same physics, from citing papers):
- Radiation-reaction force, synchrotron, guiding-centre (Hirvijoki 2015, `Hirvijoki2015_RR.pdf`, p4, eqs. 2.10-2.12): <F^p> = -gamma p (1 - xi^2)/tau_r, <F^xi> = xi (1 - xi^2)/(gamma tau_r), tau_r = 6 pi eps0 (m c)^3/(q^4 B^2) = 3c/(2 r gamma^2 Omega^2)... the text prints tau_r = 6 pi eps0 (mc)^3/(q^4 B^2), with r the classical electron radius and Omega = qB/(gamma m). Note the force grows with p_perp^2 (Sec. 3: "increases with the square of the perpendicular momentum"), which is why a steady state and a bump exist.
- Same force in the normalisation of Hesslow 2018 (`Hesslow2018_Eceff.pdf`, p6, eqs. 14-15): tau_syn^-1 = tau_c e^4 B^2/(6 pi eps0 m_e^3 c^3) ~ B_T^2/(15.44 ln Lambda_c n_e20).
- Ratio of synchrotron to collisional strength, sigma (Embreus 2016, `Embreus2016_brems.pdf`, p7, eq. 25): sigma = tau_c/tau_S = (2 eps0/(3 m_e)) B^2/(n_e ln Lambda). Choudhury 2025 (p2) uses the inverse: tau_rad-hat = 6 pi eps0 m_e^3 c^3/(e^4 B^2) divided by tau_c, equal to 10 for shot 201948 (B = 1.4 T, n_e ~ 0.5e19 m^-3).
- Location of the high-energy bump from synchrotron RR (the practical energy limit), Embreus 2016 p8, eq. 26, quoting Hirvijoki 2015: p_0S > ((1 + sigma)/sigma) (E/E_c - 1)/(1 + Zeff) (high-energy limit). The bump is not a hard cutoff: the tail decays asymptotically exponentially.
- Bremsstrahlung analogue (Embreus 2016 p8, eqs. 27-30): force balance eE - eE_c - ((1+Zeff)/(pi ln Lambda)) alpha e E_c gamma (ln 2 gamma - 1/3) = 0, giving p_0B = (pi ln Lambda/(alpha (ln 2 gamma_0 - 1/3))) (E/E_c - 1)/(1 + Zeff) (eq. 28). Bremsstrahlung beats synchrotron when n_e >~ (2 pi/(3 alpha (ln 2 gamma_0 - 1/3))) (eps0/m_e) B^2 (eq. 30). The abstract concludes synchrotron dominates in typical tokamak scenarios except at very high density, such as massive gas injection.
- Decker 2016 (`Decker2016_bump.pdf`, p13, closing remarks): any force that grows with energy "could play a similar role" as the ALD force, giving a maximum energy and a bump.
- Landreman 2014 (`Landreman2014_CODE.pdf`, abstract and Sec. 5): CODE computes the distribution and synchrotron emission; used to estimate maximum runaway energy in experiments.

## 4. Niel g(chi) and the quantum correction

- Steady-state efficiency from the Fokker-Planck flux, **eq. 2.25a-b (p187, PDF p13)**: J/P_d = {q/(Gamma (5+Z))} S_w.(d/dv)(v^3 v_par)/S_w.(d/dv)(m v^2/2), normalised J/P_d = {1/(5+Z)} S_w.(d/du)(w u^3)/S_w.(d/du)(u^2/2), u = v/v_T, w = v_par/v_T, normalisation J ~ e n v_T, P_d ~ nu n m v_T^2, nu = Gamma/v_T^3. "Not only is it efficient to push electrons in the parallel direction ... but it is almost as efficient to push them in the perpendicular direction. The precise ratio ... 4:3" (Fisch-Boozer 1980; numerics Karney-Fisch 1981, Fig. 9, p187). The efficiency formula needs only the wave-induced flux S_w, not f.
- 1D theory (p187): f = f_m(v_perp) F(w); at large w the equation is dF/dtau = d/dw[D_LH dF/dw] + (2+Z_i) d/dw[(1/w^3) dF/dw + F/w^2] - (qE/(m v)) dF/dw (eq. 2.27); steady state without E, F = C exp{-int w dw/(1 + w^3 D(w)/(2+Z))} (eq. 2.28).
- Relativistic version (p198, PDF p24): J = int S_p . d chi/dp d^3p (2.74a), P_d = int S_p . d eps/dp d^3p (2.74b), eps = (gamma - 1) m c^2; chi solves the relativistic adjoint equation C(f_m chi) = -q v_par f_m (2.75); Legendre expansion chi = mu chi_1(p) (2.76) leads to eq. 2.77-2.78 for chi_1 with Karney-Fisch 1985 finite-temperature corrections (efficiency up 10-25%). "The efficiency approaches a constant" as p -> infinity (J/P_d -> 0 for ECCD) because relativistic mass increase (p197-198).
- This is the budget the "power threshold" claims should be compared against: J/P_d (current per power) is the cost of **maintaining a momentum-space shift against collisions**; for the runaway-control problem the analogous cost is the flux through the resonance, not a current.

**Karney, Phys. Fluids 21, 1584 (1978)**, stochastic ion heating by a lower-hybrid wave (`Karney1978_LH.pdf`, 54-page typewritten report, report page n = PDF p n+1; text layer garbled, equations read from page images).
- Abstract (PDF p2): "For ions with v_perp >~ omega/k_perp the motion is stochastic for fields satisfying **E/B_0 > (1/4)(Omega_i/omega)^(1/3)(omega/k_perp)**" (checked at 250 dpi: the exponent is 1/3; E/B in velocity units).
- Derivation (report p15-16, PDF p16-17): alpha > r f(delta)/(nu |H_nu^(1)'(r)|) (eq. 46), taking f = 1/4 (constant to within 20%) alpha > r/(4 nu |H^(1)'_nu(r)|) (eq. 47); at r ~ nu, **alpha = nu^(2/3)/4 (eq. 48)**, i.e. E_0/B_0 = (1/4)(Omega/omega)^(1/3) omega/k_perp (eq. 49), with nu = omega/Omega, r = k_perp v_perp/Omega, alpha = k_perp E_0/(B_0 Omega) (so eq. 48 and 49 are the same condition). Stochastic region extends to r = (4 alpha nu)^(2/3)(2/pi)^(1/3) (eq. 50). Their threshold definition requires a fraction of phase space of order unity to be stochastic; Fukuyama et al. use the field at which a stochastic band first appears, which is why their constant is 0.15 instead of 1/4 (PDF p17). For r < nu a particle is not in resonance but can be trapped by the wave (p16, "strongly nonlinear effect of trapping").
- Mechanism (Sec. IV, PDF p11-13): islands at successive cyclotron harmonics grow with amplitude and overlap; the threshold is the field at which first-order islands plus their sub-islands overlap and destroy the island chain structure. Applicability condition eq. 85 (PDF p24): beta << r^-1 for the finite-beta correction.
- **Mapping to the runaway/helicon problem** (my reading, not in the paper): Karney's stochasticity is for a single monochromatic wave with many cyclotron harmonics at one particle, threshold alpha ~ nu^(2/3)/4. For runaways at the normal resonance the harmonics n = 0, +/-1 only are accessible (Choudhury PPCF eq. 3, PDF p8; Fulop eq. 15, only n = -1 contributes), so single-wave harmonic overlap is probably not the operative criterion; line-to-line overlap in the launched n_par spectrum (Chirikov, with trapping width 2 w_b) is. A direct Karney-type calculation for l = 1 only has not been done here.

**Karney and Fisch, Phys. Fluids 22, 1817 (1979)** (`Karney1979_currentdrive.pdf`, PPPL-1506, report page n = PDF p n+1; text layer garbled, equations read from images; the PDF text OCR gives wrong sub/superscripts)
- Eq. 1 (report p5, PDF p6): df/dt = d/dv_par D_rf(v_par) d/dv_par f + (df/dt)_c, **parallel-only diffusion** (no perpendicular QL term), (df/dt)_c linearised Fokker-Planck against fixed Maxwellian electrons and ions (Trubnikov). Z_i = 1, m_i/m_e = 1836, T_i/T_e = 1. nu_0 = lnL omega_pe^4/(2 pi n_0 v_Te^3) (eq. 2); normalised eq. 3 with D(w) = D_rf/(v_Te^2 nu_0); **step spectrum D(w) = D for w_1 < w < w_2, else 0** (eq. 4), D chosen large enough that the answer is insensitive to it (the response saturates, 2% change in J/P_d when D is doubled).
- Results (report p17-21, PDF p18-22): 1D theory J = int ... exp(-w_1^2/2) (eq. 18), P_d = ... (eq. 19) ; turn-on time 1D prediction T = 4 Delta w_1^2/alpha (eq. 20) versus numerical **T_t-o ~ 6 Delta^(1/2) w_1^2** (eq. 21, Delta = w_2 - w_1); **J/P_d = <w^2>/alpha (eq. 22), <w^2> = Delta (w_1 + w_2)/2 / ln(w_2/w_1) = (w_1^2 + w_2^2)/2 [1 + O(Delta/w_1)^2] (eq. 23), alpha = (2 + Z_i)/2; numerically J/P_d = 1.7 <w^2> (eq. 24, PDF p21-22)**, i.e. 1.7 times the 1D result with alpha = 1 used in Fisch-Boozer (1D used alpha = 1, "should be 3/2 for Z_i = 1"). Z_i dependence: 1D predicts J/P_d ratios 1 : 3/5 : 3/7 for Z_i = 1, 3, 5; 2D numerics give 1 : 0.82 : 0.71 (PDF p22).
- No stochasticity threshold appears in this paper; the "overlap" of Karney 1978 is the other one. The 1986 relativistic adjoint paper (Karney-Fisch 1986) is not in the collection.

## 9. Thesis A novelty check (PRL_SURVEY.md, Thesis A: coherent-wave runaway control saturates at a trapping-limited rate; power threshold from island overlap)

Question: do the three papers already propose trapping, nonlinear or non-quasilinear scattering, or line overlap as the mechanism for the power threshold? Method: full read of PRL and PPCF text, keyword search (trapp, nonlinear, overlap, stochas, threshold, saturat, bounce, Chirikov, island, amplitude), and reading of the relevant sections; Kang (JPP) read in the sections on trapping and the QL comparison. Short quotes only.

**Choudhury et al. PRL 136, 025101 (published).** No hits for trapping, nonlinear, overlap, island, bounce, Chirikov, or a power threshold. The word "threshold" appears only for the Aleynikov-Breizman reference title and the multi-E_c rule of thumb. Mechanism = "resonant pitch-angle scattering" + synchrotron damping with a QL Fokker-Planck solver (p3: "accounts for quasilinear diffusion in momentum space"). The power is quoted ("~150 kW") but no dependence on power is shown. **Does not propose any of the Thesis-A mechanisms.**

**Choudhury et al. PPCF 68, 105047 (published).** Keyword hits are all irrelevant except:
- PDF p10 (journal p9): "Those quasilinear simulations generally find that above some threshold wave energy density, the diffusion in RE phase space does not change drastically." This is a saturation of the QL diffusion with wave energy, stated as a property of the Guo et al. QL simulations; no trapping, no bounce frequency, no sqrt(amplitude) scaling.
- PDF p17 and p22: "Additional shots will be needed to more accurately determine the power threshold"; "Further data is needed to determine the existence of a power threshold". No mechanism offered for a threshold.
- PDF p25 (App. B): "potentially be stochasticized in phase space" -- about the wave's n_par spectrum after many passes (a broader n_par spectrum "could be beneficial"), i.e. broadening of the wave spectrum, not particle trapping or line overlap, and not tied to the threshold.
- PDF p14 (201950): "not sufficient to scatter all the REs and presumably more would have been needed", i.e. a depletion/refill argument (E-field refills 1.65 MeV per 100 ms).
**Does not propose trapping, nonlinear scattering, or line overlap. No mechanism for the threshold; the threshold itself is hedged in the published version.** (The arXiv v1 had a stronger "evidence of a power threshold" claim, also without mechanism.)

**Kang, Yoon, Cho, Yun, J. Plasma Phys. 92, E101 (2026) (the "firewall" paper; not Choudhury; `Kang2026_firewall.pdf`).** This one does propose nonlinear coherent-wave mechanisms:
- PDF p2: "For a sufficiently strong wave amplitude the particle may be trapped near resonance, at which point it undergoes acceleration in the direction opposite to its prior motion as well as in the perpendicular direction." Exact relativistic pseudo-potential analysis of an electron in a coherent R-wave plus a constant parallel E field (abstract: "becomes trapped in the resonance space"; "an externally injected R-wave can act as a firewall suppressing further runaway-electron acceleration").
- PDF p6: a trapping amplitude threshold: for E_0 = 3.55e-5 (normalised) "all electrons are resonantly trapped for b > 4.75e-4, whereas all electrons pass through resonance for b < 3.9e-4", with intermediate b depending on initial phase (b = relative wave magnetic field).
- PDF p9: "Although quasilinear theory does not apply for coherent waves ..." and the coherent R-wave pitch-angle scattering "proceeds roughly 10^6 times faster than expected from quasilinear diffusion" (tau_QL ~ 2e9 vs tau_sim ~ 1e3 in normalised units).
- PDF p10: power to compensate damping P = (|omega_i|/2 pi)(b^2 B_0^2/2 mu_0) V_p ~ 155 kW (KSTAR, B_0 = 3.5 T, n_0 = 1e19, T_e = 100 eV) as an upper bound.
- PDF p15 (references): Kang, Yoon, Cho, Yun 2024, "Fast nonlinear scattering of runaway electron beams through resonant interactions with plasma waves", Nucl. Fusion 64, 106025 (**not in the collection**) and an APS-DPP 2025 abstract (entry 33): the nonlinear/trapping route to RE control by a coherent wave has already been published by this group.
It does **not** discuss a power threshold in an experiment, line overlap among many launched lines, bandwidth requirements, collisions/radiation-reaction-limited islands, or the DIII-D data.

**Zhou et al. Phys. Scr. 84, 015501 (2011), EAST LHCD** (`Zhou2011_EASTLH.pdf`): an older RF-power threshold for runaway suppression, "when the power of LHWs is below a certain threshold, fast electrons ... can be accelerated to runaway electrons ... but when the power is high enough, fast electrons cannot transform into runaway electrons" (abstract, PDF p2); mechanism discussed is loop-voltage reduction by the driven current (PDF p2-3), not wave scattering. A precedent that "RF power threshold for runaway suppression" per se is not new; its explanation is not Thesis A.

**Zhou, Li, Liu (PKU) slides** (`Zhou.pdf` = `Zhou2025_helicon_DREAM_slides.pdf`, byte-identical): DREAM with a QL helicon term; the only "trapping" in the slides is toroidal (banana-orbit) trapped fraction raised by pitch-angle scattering (slides on scattering enhancing the trapped population), not resonance trapping. No nonlinear or overlap content.

**Verdict (Thesis A).**
1. The sentence "DIII-D shows the effect and a power threshold with no mechanism (Choudhury 2025, 2026)" in PRL_SURVEY.md Thesis A is **supported for the Choudhury papers**: neither proposes trapping, nonlinear or non-QL scattering, or line overlap. But "a power threshold" should be softened to "a hint of a power threshold in three non-ideal shots, which the published PPCF abstract no longer claims, with the ordering of the shots reversed between arXiv v1 and the published version".
2. **The "trapping-limited, non-diffusive for a coherent line" half of Thesis A is NOT novel**: Kang et al. 2026 (JPP) and Kang et al. 2024 (NF 64, 106025) show resonance trapping, a trapping amplitude threshold, and a coherent-versus-QL speed ratio of ~10^6 for runaways in a coherent R-wave with an E_parallel field. Ghai 2025 already shows non-diffusive kicks. What remains open is: the criterion for *many lines* (overlap parameter, required launch bandwidth), the inclusion of collisions and radiation reaction in the island dynamics (island vs synchrotron-limited fixed point), a quantitative amplitude threshold for DIII-D launch parameters, and the link to the observed scattering vs applied power. Karney 1978 and Pokol 2008 (QL conditions, "(i) the waves should be incoherent ... to avoid particle trapping") supply the old statement of the QL/trapping boundary, which the PRL would have to cite.
3. Claim to avoid in a paper: "no one has proposed trapping for runaway wave control". Cite Kang 2024/2026. Safer framing: combined RR + collisions + line-overlap criterion and the bandwidth law, tested against the DIII-D power data, with trapping as the mechanism of the QL breakdown.
4. Not yet read: Guo, McDevitt, Tang 2018 (PoP 25, 032504) and 2017 (PPCF 59, 044003); Kang 2024. These are the two remaining items that could contradict the verdict; the first is the "theoretical basis" of the DIII-D experiment, so a trapping/nonlinear statement in it would reduce novelty further. They are listed as missing in INDEX.md.

### 12.1 Kang, Yoon, Cho, Yun, Nucl. Fusion 64, 106025 (2024) (`Kang2024_nonlinear_scattering.pdf`)

**Correction to section 9, item 2 and the 2026-11-11 PLAN revision.** Those texts say Kang 2024 "show[s] resonance trapping, a trapping amplitude threshold, and a coherent-versus-QL speed ratio of ~10^6". That was inferred from the citation in Kang 2026, before the paper was in hand. **It is wrong.** Keyword search of the full text: zero hits for island, bounce, overlap, amplitude, Chirikov, Fokker; "trap" appears only in a reference title; "quasi" only in the introduction (p3). Trapping, the b > 4.75e-4 threshold and the 10^6 factor are in Kang 2026 (JPP) only.

- **What the paper is.** Self-generated waves. A monoenergetic RE beam (nRE = ne/8) in a cold plasma is unstable; the wave it excites scatters the beam. No wave is launched. 2D slab, 3D momentum space, 3D fields, periodic boundaries, Smilei with the Lehe solver (p5, p6).
- **Model (p3-4).** Cold-plasma parallel-propagating dispersion relation split into electrostatic, R and L brackets, eqs. (3)-(5). Harmonic Doppler resonance omega - kx U = l |omega_c|/gamma, eq. (6); l = 1, 0, -1 are normal, Cherenkov, anomalous. Timescales tau_coll eq. (7), tau_rad eq. (8), tau_accel eq. (9). Initial distributions are delta functions, eqs. (10)-(11); zero net current, eq. (12). Curvature drift eq. (13), drift length 20.7 mm in the run time, eq. (14), p6.
- **Parameters.** ne = 3e19 m^-3, Te = 100 eV, B0 = 3.5 T, E0 = 20 V/m (used only for the timescales), gamma = 21 (Table 1, p5: tau_coll 88.0 ms, tau_rad 421 ms, tau_accel 85.3 us, tau_ce 10.2 ps). Table 2 (p6): KRE = 1 and 10 MeV (gamma 3 and 21), wpe/|wce| = 0.5, Lx = 100 c/wpe, Ly = 25 c/wpe, Tsim = 600/wpe (12.2 ns), 64 particles per cell. RE density is 100x the experimental value, "to expedite and amplify the effect".
- **Collisions and radiation reaction: explicitly absent.** p5: "did not use any collision or radiation module because of the timescales involved". The argument (p4) is that wave-particle times (ps-ns) are far below tau_coll and tau_rad. So the paper tells us nothing about collisions or RR acting on the scattered beam. Appendix B scaling t_sat ~ (nRE/ne)^(-1/3), eq. (B.1), p16; the 10 MeV case is ~20x slower than this (p16).
- **Scattering rate versus quasilinear theory.** None for the scattering. The only theory comparison is the linear growth rate of the self-generated wave: measured 2 Im(w)max = 0.16 vs linear 0.21 (1 MeV, p8); 0.030 vs 0.032 (10 MeV, p10). Scattering is reported as the change of mean pitch angle (25.5 deg for 1 MeV, 18.7 deg for 10 MeV, p7, p9) and the fractional change of the parallel kinetic-energy flux, -0.32 (1 MeV, p9) and -0.34 (10 MeV, p10).
- **Which waves.** 1 MeV: Cherenkov resonance, electrostatic wave gives parallel diffusion, then whistler gives perpendicular diffusion (p7-8). 10 MeV: anomalous Doppler, whistler first (p9). Figures 6 and 10.
- **Multiple lines, spectral width, overlap.** Not analysed. The dispersion plots show resonance signals plus "unpredicted signals around the harmonic Doppler resonance signals" that are left "to be studied in the future" (p12). No spectral width, no overlap criterion, no bandwidth.
- **Power estimate.** None. The conclusion says external wave injection "will be studied" in the future (p12).
- **Net.** Not a coherent-wave control paper. It is a beam-driven instability paper. It is background for Kang 2026 and says nothing on any of our three parts.

### 12.2 Guo, McDevitt, Tang, Phys. Plasmas 25, 032504 (2018) (`Guo2018_whistler_control.pdf`)

- **Model (p3-6).** Relativistic Fokker-Planck in (p, xi), slab, eq. (20): acceleration + pinch by E, Coulomb collisions (test-particle only), synchrotron damping C_rad, knock-on source (Chiu), plus the quasilinear wave operator C(f) of eq. (10), with D_pp, D_pxi, D_xixi given by eq. (11) and the resonance delta function eq. (12), n = +/-1 harmonics. Wave amplitude eq. (14); spectrum eq. (15): E_{-1,k} = E0 delta(k_perp - k_perp0)/(...) exp[-(k_par - k_par0)^2/(2 Dk^2)], a **single narrow Gaussian line**, Dk = 0.05, k_perp0 = 0.1 (p5). Simplified whistler dispersion omega = k|k_par| w, eqs. (16)-(17), w = (wce/wpe)^2 = 1 (p5). Resonant gamma for anomalous and normal Doppler, eqs. (18)-(19): normal Doppler works at any energy, anomalous needs gamma > 4 for k_par = 0.5 (p5).
- **Collisions and RR: yes, self-consistently, but in the quasilinear regime.** Collisions, synchrotron damping and avalanche source are all in the same kinetic equation as the wave term (p6, p8). The control mechanism is the O-X vortex of Guo 2017: waves raise the pitch at p between pX and pO, synchrotron damping then balances E at lower energy, eq. (21): p_O ~ sqrt[(E/alpha)(1-Dxi)/(2Dxi - Dxi^2)] (p7). Result: pO falls from 20 to 3.5 (factor 5.7, p7), X point unchanged when the resonance is well above pX; moving it down (k_par0 = 0.3 to 0.7) re-merges O and X and the control is lost (p7-8, Fig. 5).
- **Parameters.** E = 3.0 Ec, alpha = tau_c/tau_s = 0.2 (also 0.05), vt = 0.1c, omega_pe tau_c = 1e10, omega_pe = omega_ce (p5-6). Wave in Fig. 2/3: k_par0 = 0.2, Dk = 0.05, E0 = 2.5e-11 (normalised to ne me c^2), frequency about 0.05 omega_ce (p6).
- **Amplitude.** "dB ~ 1e-5 B0" corresponds to integral E d3k ~ 1e-10 (p4, page image checked); the summary says "dB < 1e-4 B0" suffices (p10-11). There is **no amplitude threshold and no trapping**: the whole treatment assumes "small amplitude waves" and quasilinear diffusion (p4: "long time behavior ... quasilinear diffusion process").
- **Overlap and spectrum.** One line. Widths and positions are scanned (k_par0 in Fig. 5, Dk fixed), but only to place the resonance relative to pX. No overlap criterion.
- **Avalanche.** With the knock-on source the same vortex limits secondaries to below 2.5 MeV (Fig. 7, p8); avalanche growth rate is reduced most near k_par0 = 0.3 and recovers at higher k_par0 (Fig. 8, p9-10).
- **Power estimate, Sec. VI (p9).** P = Gamma W_k. ITER (ne = 1e20, a = 2 m, R = 6.2 m): total wave energy 0.18 J; collisional damping in a 10 eV plasma at B = 5.3 T is ~1e-4 omega_ce; damping of the Fig. 3 wave ~5e-6 omega_ce; "about 800 kW", inside the 20 MW ITER lower-hybrid system. Figs. 9-10 give damping versus (ne, Te). They call for "a more accurate and self-consistent calculation" of propagation and dissipation (p9).
- **Stated conclusion on wave-driven control (p10-11).** Whistlers of dB < 1e-4 B0 can hold the runaway energy "below a few MeV" without reducing the current, with a power requirement "well below" ITER RF capability. Toroidal trapping "is expected to make the mechanism more effective" (p11).
- **Stated open item that matters to us (p8-9).** "Extension of this technique" (the adjoint method of rf current drive) to energy control by wave injection "remains to be carried out".

### 12.3 Guo, McDevitt, Tang, PPCF 59, 044003 (2017) (`Guo2017_phasespace.pdf`)

- **No waves.** Keyword search finds none (the only "resonan" hit is "overlap between p ~ 0.2-0.5 me c"; "trap" none). Not a control paper; it is the baseline for Guo 2018.
- **Model.** Relativistic Fokker-Planck eq. (1): f_t - E(xi f_p + (1-xi^2) f_xi/p) = C(f) + C_rad(f), slab, test-particle collisions; synchrotron force from gyromotion eq. (8) with tau_s = 6 pi eps0 me^3 c^3/(e^4 B^2) (also a toroidal-curvature term). Fluxes Gamma_p, Gamma_xi eqs. (14)-(15), model pitch distribution eqs. (16)-(19).
- **Result.** Instead of the 1D "attractor", the (p, xi) flow forms a vortex with an O point (Gamma_p = Gamma_xi = 0) and an X point near p ~ 2, xi ~ -0.8; the vortex exists at fields well below the bump-on-tail onset; bump at p_b ~ p_O/1.55; bump width set by the O-X separation (p3, p8-10). From 2.0 Ec to 2.5 Ec the population in the vortex grows by almost ten orders of magnitude (p8).
- **Conclusions on wave-driven runaway control.** None. Wave control appears only in Guo 2018.

### 12.4 Guo 2018 and the DIII-D papers

Guo 2018 has no amplitude threshold and no E0 scan. Its only amplitude statement is that at E > ~100 Ec "the intensity of the whistler waves needs to be increased proportionally" because the pinching toward xi = 1 grows linearly with E (p7). Choudhury PPCF's sentence (section 9) that QL simulations "find that above some threshold wave energy density, the diffusion ... does not change drastically" is attributed there to Guo et al.; no such statement is in the Guo 2018 text (keyword search: threshold, amplitude, saturat). Treat that attribution as unverified; it may refer to other QL runs.

## 13. Karney and Fisch, Phys. Fluids 29, 180 (1986) (`Karney1986_currentdrive.pdf`; scan, equations read from images, PDF p2-5)

- **Setting.** Uniform electron-ion plasma, constant E parallel to B, wave-induced flux S(v, t); f = f_m + f_1, linearised Boltzmann eq. (1), p3 (journal p181). Collision operator in the high-velocity limit, C(f) = Gamma[(1/v^2) d(f)/dv + ((1+Z)/(2v^3)) d/dmu (1-mu^2) df/dmu] (journal p181): pitch-angle scattering and friction, **no energy diffusion, no radiation reaction**.
- **Normalisation.** Runaway velocity v_r from friction = qE; tau = nu_r t, u = v/v_r. Eq. (2): df_1/dtau + D(f_1) = -d/du . S, with D = -d/du_par - (1/u^2) d/du - ((1+Z)/(2u^3)) d/dmu (1-mu^2) d/dmu (p4). E normalises away; only Z remains.
- **Langevin form.** Eqs. (4)-(5): du/dtau = -1/u^2 - mu, dmu/dtau = A(tau) - (1-mu^2)/u, with stochastic A of mean -(1+Z)mu/u^3 and variance (1+Z)(1-mu^2)/u^3 (p5). This is the Monte Carlo formulation of Karney-Fisch 1979.
- **Adjoint formalism.** For a moment H(tau) = int h_0(u) f_1 d3u (h_0 = u_par gives current), define h(u', tau) = int h_0(u) g(u, tau; u') d3u, eq. (6); then H(tau) = int_0^tau dtau' int d3u S(u, tau') . d/du h(u, tau - tau'), eq. (7); h obeys the adjoint equation dh/dtau + D*(h) = 0, h(tau = 0) = h_0, eq. (8), D* = d/du_par + (1/u^2) d/du - ((1+Z)/(2u^3)) d/dmu (1-mu^2) d/dmu, with h(u = 0) = 0 (journal p182-183, PDF p4-5). One adjoint solve gives the moment for **any** wave source S. Boundary treatment: spherical domain u < u_b, characteristics of (2) enter on Sigma_in and leave on Sigma_out, those of (8) the reverse (p5). Energy diffusion would turn the problem parabolic (p5).
- **What the rest gives (abstract and section list, PDF p2-3; not transcribed).** Runaway probability and current Green's function from the numerical adjoint solution, current density in a two-function form, power coupling to poloidal field (rf ramp-up), circuit equations for ray-tracing and transport codes.
- **Use for us.** The adjoint gives the response of a chosen moment (current, energy flux, runaway rate) to **any** S, so the gradient of an objective with respect to the launched spectrum costs one adjoint solve. Guo 2018 (p8-9) says this extension to energy control "remains to be carried out". Limits: KF's D* has no RR, no energy diffusion, a constant E, and v >> v_t; the RE problem needs C_rad and the avalanche source in D*, which breaks the E-normalisation (alpha and E/Ec become parameters).

## 14. Mirzaie et al., Nat. Photon. 18, 1212 (2024) (`Mirzaie2024_nlCompton.pdf`)

- **chi reached.** chi = sqrt(F_mu_nu p_nu)/(E_S m c) = E_r/E_S (p1). Maximum chi ~ 0.46 (abstract p1; Shot 4 of Supp. Table 1, p4: 2.2 GeV rms electron energy, a0 = 14, mean photon energy 73 MeV, nonlinearity order n = 420). Laser: CoReLS 4 PW, a0 up to 14, peak intensity 4e20 W/cm^2, 30 deg noncollinear geometry (p2). Electron beams 1-3 GeV (up to 3.5 GeV available, p2). Typical shots have chi ~ 0.13-0.32 (p4). 43% of 70+ scattering events had a0 between 8 and 12 (p4). Highest photon energy 670 MeV, Shot 5: 2.8 GeV, a0 = 10 (p4).
- **Radiation-reaction observable.** The measured quantity is the gamma-ray spectrum (cutoff beyond the linear-Compton limit, critical energy from a fit dN/dE ~ E^(-2/3) exp(-E/Ec), p3) and its mean photon energy versus a0, compared with the quantum synchrotron emission rate "accounting for the classical radiation reaction", and with OSIRIS QED-PIC with Monte Carlo emission and "quantum radiation reaction" (p4, Methods p7). So RR enters only through the models that reproduce the spectra; the electron energy loss is not the headline observable, and the paper does not claim a chi at which RR is isolated. The authors state agreement validates the LCFA (p5).
- **Relevance.** chi < 1 here, so the quantum correction to RR is modest; useful only as an experimental anchor for the classical/quantum loss ratio g(chi) used in the program. Not relevant to the tokamak thesis.
